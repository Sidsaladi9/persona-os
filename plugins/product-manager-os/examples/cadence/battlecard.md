# Battlecard — vs. Geekbot

**Updated:** 2026-07-14 · **Owner:** Marcus Feld (Sales) · **Next review:** 2026-10-14
**We face them in:** 34% of competitive deals · **Win rate vs. them:** 58% (14 of 24, trailing 2 quarters)

## The frame (say this early)
> "Geekbot collects check-ins. The question is what happens after — eleven paragraphs land in your Slack and somebody still has to read all of them to find the one that matters. We're built for the reading, not the collecting."

## Why we win
| Reason | Say it like this | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| Blocker detection surfaces the one that matters | "You get a digest with the stuck person at the top, in their own words. Not eleven messages in a channel." | 63% of teams see a real blocker in week 1; 18% dismissal rate |
| Built for managers of 20+, not squads of 6 | "Their sweet spot is one small team. Ours starts where reading everything stops being possible." | Median Cadence team: 47 seats. Median Geekbot team: 9 |
| Digest is a place, not a message | "Their output lives in a channel and scrolls away. Ours is a page you open at 8:40 and close at 8:46." | Dana Whitfield ref call |

## Why we lose *(read this before you argue)*
| Reason | Honest reframe | When to concede outright |
|---|---|---|
| They're cheaper at small size — free under 10 users | "Under ten people you're all in one room. Honestly, use the free thing." | Team under 10 — disqualify, don't discount |
| Their Slack setup is genuinely faster (about 2 min) | "Theirs is faster to switch on. Ours takes fifteen minutes because it's reading the content, not just relaying it." | Buyer explicitly wants zero-config and has 8 people |
| More integrations on the directory page | "They integrate wider; we integrate deeper into Slack. If you need Jira sync today, we're a quarter out." | Jira sync is a hard requirement |

## Three traps
**1. "When someone on my team is blocked, how do I find out?"**
- **What they'll say:** the check-in shows up in the channel and you read it.
- **Why it lands:** it's structural. Their model is relay, not read. Adding detection would mean processing content they've built a business on not touching.

**2. "What does this look like on a Monday with 40 people checking in?"**
- **What they'll say:** you can split by channel, or filter.
- **Why it lands:** the answer is always "organize it yourself," which is the job the buyer was trying to hand over.

**3. "If nobody reacts to my engineers' updates, what stops them from quietly stopping?"**
- **What they'll say:** reminders and nudges.
- **Why it lands:** nudges address compliance, not the reason people stop. Every buyer with 20+ people has watched this happen and recognizes it immediately.

## Objection handling
**"We already use Geekbot and it's fine."**
> "Then don't switch on my account. One question — how long does your manager spend in the check-ins each morning, and do they feel confident they haven't missed anything? If the answer's yes, you're genuinely fine."

**"They're cheaper."**
> "They are, under ten people. At 50 seats we're $2,880 a year and they're about $2,300 — the gap is roughly one engineer-day a month. If the digest saves your manager fifteen minutes a day, it's paid for in the first week."

**"They have more integrations."**
> "True — they integrate wider. We integrate deeper into the one place your team already is. If Jira sync is a must-have today, I'd rather tell you we're a quarter out than sell you a maybe."

**"We'd have to migrate our history."**
> "You would, and it's a real cost. We import Geekbot history via CSV — takes about an hour and we do it with you on a call."

## Disqualify fast when
- **Team is under 10 people.** They should use the free tier. Say so — it buys credibility and they come back at 25.
- **Jira sync is a stated hard requirement this quarter.** We lose, and spending three calls proving it wastes everyone's time.
- **Buyer wants individual productivity scoring.** We will never ship it. Walk.

## Proof points
- **Customer:** Northwind Labs (90 eng, switched from Geekbot Q1) — manager time in check-ins dropped from 22 minutes to 6, and they caught a three-day blocker in week one that nobody had escalated.
