# Cadence now tells your manager you're stuck — before you have to say it
### For engineering managers running async teams: the blockers that used to surface a week late now surface the same morning.

**SAN FRANCISCO — September 8, 2026** — Cadence today announced the AI Blocker Detector, which lets engineering managers see which of their people are stuck the morning it happens, without anyone having to raise a hand. Available to all Team and Enterprise plans at no extra cost.

## The problem
Async check-ins solved the standup meeting and created a new problem: nobody reads them. A manager with eleven reports gets eleven paragraphs every morning, and the one that matters is buried in the seventh.

Across Cadence teams, the median manager opens the digest 71% of days but spends 40 seconds in it. In our interviews, the same sentence came back nine times: *"I find out someone was blocked when the sprint slips."* The cost is measurable — teams that hit an unsurfaced multi-day blocker are the ones that stop checking in, and 39% of teams don't survive to a qualifying week two.

## What we built
Cadence now reads the check-ins your team already writes and flags the ones that describe a blocker — someone waiting on a review, stuck on an environment, blocked on a decision that isn't coming. The flag appears at the top of the digest with the person's own words, so the manager sees the sentence, not a score.

Managers can mark a flag as not-real in one click, and the detector adjusts. Nothing about the check-in changes for the person writing it — they write what they always wrote.

Turn it on in Team Settings. It reads the check-ins you're already collecting, so there's nothing to configure and no new habit for the team.

# FAQ

## Customer questions
**Does this read my team's check-ins?**
Yes — the same check-ins already shown to the manager in the digest. We don't store blocker text and we don't train on your content.

**What does it cost?**
Nothing. It's included on Team ($4/user/mo) and Enterprise.

**What if it flags things that aren't blockers?**
Dismiss it in one click. We treat dismissal as the accuracy signal — silence isn't taken as agreement.

## Internal questions
**Why are we the right ones to build this?**
We already have the check-in text and the manager's attention every morning. Nobody else has both.

**What's the biggest risk, and what would we do if it happens?**
False positives train managers to ignore the top of the digest — the exact spot we're spending our credibility on. Mitigation: ship at high-confidence threshold only, watch dismissal rate, and pull back the threshold rather than the feature.

**What are we explicitly NOT building in v1?**
No blocker assignment, no resolution tracking, no Slack alerts, no cross-team rollups. This surfaces; it doesn't manage.

**What has to be true for this to work?** *(the riskiest assumption)*
That managers act on a surfaced blocker rather than noting it. Cheapest test: for two weeks, hand-flag blockers for 20 teams and measure whether the flagged check-in gets a reply within a day. No model required.

**What does this cost to build and run?**
Roughly 6 engineer-weeks, plus $0.02–0.05 per team per day in inference at current volume.

**What are we saying no to by doing this?**
The Jira integration slips a quarter.

**What's the thing we're least sure about?**
Whether "manager notices sooner" actually moves week-2 survival, or whether the teams that churn were leaving for reasons a blocker flag never touches. Our retention thesis rests on it and we have not tested it.
